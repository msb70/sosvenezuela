HUMANITARIAN NEEDS                                                         SUMMARY ANALYSIS OF PROGRAMME
Four months after the earthquakes, the response is progressively
                                                                           RESPONSE
shifting towards early recovery while maintaining essential
humanitarian assistance for families who remain displaced or               Water, sanitation and hygiene
continue to face significant needs due to housing losses and
disruptions to basic services. The continued use of some schools as
temporary accommodation is also placing pressure on education
infrastructure and affecting the full return of children and adolescents
to learning.
Authorities and humanitarian partners are supporting the voluntary
transition of affected families from temporary camps to safer and
more sustainable accommodation. As of 11 October, 18,055 people
remained in 86 transitional camps, while 7,336 people had been
relocated and 30 camps had closed .4 Later in October, the
Government reported the delivery of 500 transitional housing units
in Ciudad Caribia, La Guaira, with an initial 400 families ,
approximately 1,750 people, moving from temporary camps and
tents into the new accommodation.5 These developments reflect
the progressive transition from emergency accommodation towards
more sustainable housing solutions, although significant temporary
accommodation needs remain.

                                    Damián, 13, his aunt Mercedes and their family share a hug by the sea in La Guaira,
                                    Venezuela, on September 8, 2026.

                                    Damián, 13, lost his mother in the 24 June earthquakes. He now
                                    lives with his aunt Mercedes and her family at the temporary camp in
                                    the José María Vargas sports complex in La Guaira. His sister María,
                                    10, is staying with relatives in Puerto la Cruz for now. Mercedes is
                                    grieving her sister and took both children into her care. She sought
                                    help from child protection services to formalize the placement. With
                                    UNICEF support, Fundainil runs a child-friendly space at the camp. It
                                    offers psychosocial support through play, sport and art, and gives
                                    caregivers positive parenting guidance to help families adjust and
                                    prevent separation. Wendy Torres, Child Protection Officer at
                                    UNICEF Venezuela, stresses that supporting grieving children also
                                    means supporting those who care for them.
