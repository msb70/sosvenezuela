Movement Trends Spotlight #1

    Cuban Flows through LAC
    Cuban migration dynamics in Latin America and the Caribbean continued to diversify during the first half of 2026, with
    evidence pointing to a growing concentration of movements and settlement processes in South America. Brazil received
    more than 17,000 asylum applications from Cuban nationals during the first five months of the year, making it one of the
    principal destinations in the region. Uruguay also recorded a strongly positive migration balance, suggesting sustained
    arrivals and longer-term settlement among Cuban migrants. At the same time, countries across Central and northern South
    America continued to play distinct roles within broader movement corridors. Guatemala and Colombia remained important
    transit locations for onward travel, while Panama maintained characteristics of both a transit and destination country. These
    patterns suggest that Cuban mobility is increasingly distributed across a range of routes and migration modalities rather
    than concentrated along a single northbound flow. Despite these evolving routes, protection risks remain significant. Deaths
    and disappearances of Cuban migrants continued to be reported along migration corridors during the reporting period,
    highlighting the persistence of dangerous journeys even as overall transit volumes through traditional routes declined.27

Movement Trends Spotlight #2

    Haitian Flows through LAC
    During the first half of 2026, Haitian migration in the region increasingly relied on regular pathways. Within South America,
    Haitian mobility was shaped largely by a family reunification programme linking Haiti and Brazil through charter flights.
    Since April 2026, more than 2,600 Haitian nationals have arrived in Brazil through these organized channels, primarily
    to southern and southeastern states. These movements illustrate how family reunification mechanisms are structuring
    part of contemporary Haitian mobility, distinguishing this route from the predominantly irregular movements observed
    elsewhere in the region.28

Movement Trends Spotlight #3

    Forced returns to Central America
    Forced return dynamics in Central America are changing. According to available data, the profile of those returning has
    shifted toward refugees and migrants with deeper ties to the United States, including many who spent years abroad and
    now face the challenge of rebuilding livelihoods, family connections and community ties after return. Research conducted
    by IOM and the Migration Policy Institute in Guatemala suggests that economic reintegration remains the central concern
    for most returnees. Many arrive with work experience acquired abroad, particularly in construction, services and skilled
    trades, and express strong interest in formal employment, entrepreneurship and certification of skills gained overseas.
    Yet significant obstacles persist. Returnees frequently report outstanding migration-related debt, loss of assets, limited
    awareness of available support programmes, and difficulties transferring experience obtained in the United States into
    employment opportunities in Guatemala. Employers, meanwhile, often require formal credentials that do not reflect
    migrants’ practical experience. Additionally, a growing share of returnees originates from the Western Highlands and
    Indigenous communities, increasing pressure on areas that are already highly dependent on remittances and where formal
    employment opportunities remain limited. The findings suggest that successful reintegration will depend not only on
    reception assistance, but also on longer-term access to employment, skills recognition, local economic opportunities, and
    community-based support. As enforcement patterns continue to evolve, return is increasingly becoming a development,
    labour-market and social-cohesion issue rather than solely a migration-management challenge.35

   Scope and coverage
   This report draws on a range of governmental, inter-agency and operational data sources to analyse mobility trends across
   Latin America and the Caribbean during the first half of 2026. It covers mixed migration movements of all nationalities,
   including regular and irregular migration, returns, asylum movements, with special attention to protection risks experienced
   by migrants in route. The analysis incorporates evidence from IBC partners, R4V National and Sub-Regional Platforms,
   national authorities, and mobility monitoring systems. Coverage varies by country and source; the report highlights the
   most significant regional developments and emerging trends rather than providing exhaustive country reporting.
