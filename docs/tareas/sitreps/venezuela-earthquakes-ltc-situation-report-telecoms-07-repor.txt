                  Situation Overview
                  Two powerful earthquakes of magnitude 7.2 and 7.5 struck north-central Venezuela on 24 June,
                  causing widespread destruction across La Guaira, Caracas, Aragua, Carabobo, Miranda, Yaracuy and
                  surrounding areas. Nine weeks after the earthquakes, the Venezuela response is transitioning from
                  emergency life-saving operations to recovery, with authorities progressively closing 107 temporary
                  operational shelter camps that host approximately 24,500 people.

                  Affected families continue to face overlapping needs for food, shelter, healthcare, safe water,
                  sanitation, protection and livelihood support. Heavy rainfall has further affected infrastructure and
                  services and increased the risks facing households in temporary or inadequate shelter. The
                  earthquakes have also generated a major recovery and reconstruction challenge. United Nations
                  Development Programme (UNDP) initially estimated approximately USD 6.7 billion in direct physical
                  damage, equivalent to about six percent of Gross Domestic Product (GDP), while a broader United
                  Nations Office for Disaster Risk Reduction (UNDRR) assessment placed housing and infrastructure

              The response is steadily moving from the acute emergency phase towards a prolonged combination of
              humanitarian assistance, service restoration and recovery. Immediate priorities are to sustain food and
              cash-based assistance; provide safe and dignified shelter; restore water, sanitation, health, education,
              electricity and telecommunications services; strengthen disease surveillance and protection
              mechanisms; and support livelihood recovery. Predictable and flexible funding is urgently required to
              prevent assistance gaps and ensure that the most vulnerable households are not left behind as
              international attention declines.



              Telecommunications Overview
              Over two months after twin magnitude 7.2 and 7.5 earthquakes struck north-central Venezuela on 24
              June, telecommunications services have progressively recovered but remain uneven and vulnerable.
              Telecommunications services in affected areas, including Mobile Network Operators (MNOs) and
              Internet Service Providers (ISPs), appear to have broadly returned to pre-earthquake conditions. Some
              online reports state that remaining connectivity gaps are understood to reflect pre-existing coverage
              limitations rather than new earthquake-related disruption, pending official confirmation from the
              National Telecommunications Commission (CONATEL).

              Reliable connectivity remains essential for coordinating humanitarian operations, sharing emergency
              information, reconnecting separated families, accessing digital services and enabling affected
              communities to communicate their needs. While Venezuela's main telecommunications providers,
              including Movistar, Digitel, CANTV and most Internet Service Providers (ISP), continued operating
              nationwide, localized outages and reduced network performance persist in some earthquake-affected
              areas. Service providers and authorities continue efforts to restore damaged infrastructure and
              address remaining connectivity gaps.

              The recovery of telecommunications services remains closely linked to the restoration of the national
              electricity network. Although power supply has improved considerably in several affected areas,
              including La Guaira, where authorities reported 98 percent restoration of electricity services by late
              July, ongoing damage to key generation and transmission infrastructure continues to affect the
              resilience of both power and communications systems.



              LTC Telecoms Activities
              Coordination

              The inter-agency LTC Telecoms Coordinator is supporting preparations for the phased closure of
              temporary operational shelter camps where inter-agency telecommunications services continue to be
              provided, ensuring service wind-down is aligned with camp closure timelines and humanitarian
              operational needs. Operational priorities are now focused on the safe and accountable
              decommissioning of internet connectivity and charging services, follow-up to the La Guaira health-
              centre assessment, handover of donated Starlink terminals and the transition of remaining
              telecommunications coordination into regular inter-agency country operations.

              The Government of Venezuela is shifting from emergency coordination to recovery and reconstruction
              implementation, which will shape the scale, duration and staffing requirements of international

              Internet connectivity

              Through a combination of Starlink connectivity and the Internet Service for Communities solution,
              developed jointly by emergency.lu, CONATEL, WFP and local ISP G-Network, LTC Telecoms continues
              to provide internet services for approximately 21,000 humanitarian responders and affected people
              across Caraballeda, Polideportivo, and César Nieves Stadium. The community internet service enables
              people currently sheltered in temporary camps to stay connected with family members, access
              information, and use essential digital services. The platform is available across camps using the same
              login credentials.

              The inter-agency LTC Telecoms Coordinator is coordinating with G-Network ISP, Grupo Telefónica and
              Global LTC Telecoms partner emergency.lu to decommission partner-provided community internet
              access, solar power solutions and ICT device-charging kiosks. Planning covers orderly shutdown
              procedures, equipment recovery and potential reuse, with implementation tied to confirmed camp
              closure dates.

              Joint assessment

              At the request of the Ministry of Health, WFP engineering personnel, World Health Organization (WHO)
              staff and the LTC team completed a multifunctional assessment of health centres in La Guaira from 13
              to 25 August. The assessment examined operational constraints affecting health service delivery and
              is informing recommendations to strengthen critical support systems, including warehousing,
              electricity supply, internet connectivity and broader infrastructure. Findings are being consolidated for
              review and recommendations. The findings will help inform recovery planning and identify areas where
              technical telecommunications and logistics support may still be required. Subject to approval of the
              recommendations, any follow-up support will be taken forward by the WFP CO as LTC lead agency,
              through regular programming rather than the emergency response, as several identified needs are
              longer-term in nature or pre-date the earthquakes emergency.

              Information Management

              LTC Telecoms continues to maintain its dedicated emergency operations page, providing operational
              updates, situation reports and coordination information.

              LTC Telecoms and the International Telecommunication Union (ITU) continue to use the Disaster
              Connectivity Map (DCM), integrated into LogIE for Venezuela, to monitor connectivity conditions and
              support operational planning.



              Funding
              Donor engagement remains ongoing as humanitarian funding requirements are consolidated under the
              forthcoming Flash Appeal.
