International Medical Corps has provided 8,716 health consultations at our
Emergency Medical Team (EMT) Type 1 Fixed facility in Catia La Mar, La
Guaira state, since beginning services on July 11. The epidemiological profile of patients has shifted over the last few
weeks, with our staff seeing an increase in infectious diseases—including vector-borne illnesses and viral infections, likely
due to seasonal environmental changes. Since the last reporting period, infectious-disease consultations—primarily
affecting adults and children over 5—increased by more than 46%. As a result, International Medical Corps has increased
measures to screen for and detect endemic diseases such as dengue and leptospirosis, and has continued providing
health-promotion messaging to reduce transmission of communicable disease.

As part of our efforts to support early recovery and strengthen
service continuity, International Medical Corps is installing an
emergency response field unit in a hospital in Caracas that
has been inoperable since the earthquake. The unit will help
provide essential healthcare services in an urban,
underserved community where access to care has been
disrupted for nearly four months. To enhance our ability to
provide treatment to field-unit patients, International Medical
Corps is conducting minor repairs to the hospital’s annex to
establish a temporary pharmacy for safe storage of
medicines.
Since deploying the first of our three mobile medical units
(MMUs) on July 1, International Medical Corps has provided
12,017 health consultations in remote and hard to reach
communities in earthquake-affected areas. During this period,
the MMUs reported similar epidemiological trends to those at
the EMT, with outreach prioritizing the screening and
detection of viral and vector borne illness. Home visits remain
a critical service offering of our teams to provide accessible
services to homebound individuals. Our MMUs, which
continue to work with the MoH and community leaders to
ensure continuity of care, have so far conducted 174 visits to
64 communities.

Our MHPSS team also continues to provide key training to staff at health facilities we support, including “Basic
Psychosocial Skills” and “Caring for Caregivers.” In the Basic Psychosocial Skills course, health personnel learn how to
provide supportive communication, practical assistance, recognize concerns requiring additional support, and make safe
referrals. The Caring for Caregivers course helps responders reduce the risk of prolonged stress and burnout while
supporting well-being, as they transition to providing other services for the community. To date, our team has trained 39
healthcare workers and community leaders.
To increase access to safe water, International Medical Corps’ Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) team has
delivered more than 1.8 million liters of clean water across communities, providing 20,000 liters of clean water per day to
four camps and two communities collectively hosting nearly 3,000 internally displaced people and community members. In
four of these distribution sites, International Medical Corps is the only WASH provider and the only source of safe water.
Every week, we also provide 10,000 liters of clean water to another transitional camp hosting approximately 3,300 people.
To ensure access to sanitation facilities, our WASH team maintains 36 temporary mobile toilets and six showers in the
transitional camps and health facilities.
At the EMT and across five camps, our WASH team continues to distribute hygiene and dignity kits to vulnerable patients
and community members, so far distributing 885 household hygiene kits, 59 individual hygiene kits, 159 dignity kits, 109
non-food item kits and 200 water survival boxes to 4,125 people. During these distributions, we provide training to support
the safe and effective use of kit contents. To date, we have conducted 59 health- and hygiene-promotion sessions at the
EMT and in surrounding communities, reaching 1,007 people on topics that have included safe water, water
contamination, waterborne diseases and general hygiene.
